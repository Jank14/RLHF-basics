1 · Same prompt, many answers

GRPO samples a group of answers to one prompt. In the notebook all 128 samples start from "This is", so the whole batch is one group.

2 · The group is the baseline

Advantage = (r − mean) / std over the group: the same normalize_reward you already wrote. Above-average answers get pushed up, below-average down.

3 · Change one reward

Press + on one answer: every advantage moves, because the mean and std of the group changed.

4 · Same credit on every token

The answer's advantage is copied to all its tokens (einops.repeat(advantages, "b -> b g")). A critic, as in PPO, would instead give credit where the forecast moved: mostly the tokens with periods.

5 · What disappears

No value head, no value loss, no compute_advantages. Objective = clipped term + entropy − KL. Cheaper, and a fair "usual" because every answer had the same prompt.

1 · Two full copies is too many

The RLHF trainer keeps the model being trained and a frozen reference copy, plus optimizer state. For big models that doesn't fit.

2 · Freeze W, learn A and B

Every original weight W stays frozen. LoRA adds x·A·B · α/r, with A (din × r) and B (r × dout). Grey = frozen, coral = trained. Move the rank slider.

3 · How small?

The notebook puts LoRA on WQ, WK, WV, WO of every layer. With r = 4 on GPT-2 small that's about 1.9M trainable numbers: ~1.5% of 124M.

4 · B starts at zero

A is random, B = 0, so x·A·B = 0: at step 0 the model is exactly the original.

5 · The reference for free

Switch the LoRA hooks off and you have the original model back. So self.ref_model = self.model: one copy in memory.

Tab 1: one round of VPG: returns, the "usual", the score, then a gradient step and new games. Tab 2: PPO's importance weight and clip on a single action.

Return from step t in each game (Gt = rewards still to come)

Average return J over rounds (what VPG maximises)

1 · Four games, one policy

Same weights, different games: the pole fell after 5, 40, 120 and 15 steps. Each bar is Gt: the +1s still to come from step t.

2 · The usual = average over games at this step

The dashed line is VPG's "usual" bt = average of Gt over the games. Move the slider: the usual changes with t, but not with the state each game is in.

3 · Score = Gt − usual

Above the line → that action was better than usual → make it more likely. Below → less likely. Short games get negative scores early on.

4 · One gradient step

θ ← θ + α · mean[∇ log π(a|s) · score]. Press Gradient step: the policy changes, we play new games, and the old ones are thrown away.

5 · Repeat

Each round the average return J climbs (bottom chart). That is all of VPG: play, score, step, repeat. (Toy simulation, not a real CartPole.)

Curve: the clipped objective min(r·A, clip(r, 0.8, 1.2)·A) as πnew moves. Flat part = gradient 0 = stop pushing.

1 · The batch was played by πold

"push right" had probability 0.40 when the game was played. After a gradient step it is 0.50.

2 · Importance weight r = πnew / πold

r = 0.50 / 0.40 = 1.25. Re-weighting old games by r tells us how the new policy would score, without playing again.

3 · Objective r · A

Positive A: raising πnew raises r·A. Keep stepping on the same batch and r keeps growing…

4 · The clip

Past r = 1.2 (for A > 0) the curve goes flat: no more reward for pushing. Drag πnew above 0.48 and watch "gradient: 0".

5 · Bad actions

Set A negative: now the flat part is below r = 0.8. A bad action can be pushed down to 0.8× its old probability per batch, no further.
